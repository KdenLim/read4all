Part One · Discover

5 Malaysian Authors You Should Know

Malaysia has produced remarkable storytellers whose books have won some of the world's biggest literary prizes. Author photos and book covers below are sourced from official author sites, publisher pages, and Goodreads - credited beneath each image.

Hanna Alkaf

Fantasy · YA · Mystery

A Kuala Lumpur-based novelist with a journalism degree from Northwestern University, Alkaf writes unapologetically Malaysian young adult and middle-grade fiction (Alkaf, n.d.).

Also known for: Queen of the Tiles · The Hysterical Girls of St. Bernadette's · The Girl and the Ghost

Why start here: The Weight of Our Sky follows an OCD teenager, Melati, trying to survive the real 13 May 1969 riots in Kuala Lumpur while searching for her missing mother. It's a rare book that treats a national trauma and a mental health condition with equal seriousness - tense, moving, and unmistakably Malaysian. It won the Freeman Award for YA/High School Literature (Tatler Asia, n.d.).

Photo: hannaalkaf.com

Tash Aw

Literary Fiction

Born in Taipei and raised in Kuala Lumpur, Aw is now based in London. His fiction has been translated into 23 languages and has won the Whitbread and Commonwealth Prizes and an O. Henry Award (BookBrowse, n.d.).

Also known for: The Harmony Silk Factory · Five Star Billionaire

Why start here: We, the Survivors is narrated by Ah Hock, a Malaysian man recounting to a journalist how he ended up serving prison time for killing a migrant worker. Told as a long confession, it quietly unpacks class, race, and the gap between the "Malaysian Dream" and the reality of low-wage labour (CBC Radio, 2019).

Photo: Goodreads

Tan Twan Eng

Historical Fiction

Born in Penang and once a lawyer in Kuala Lumpur, Tan became the first Malaysian writer recognised by the Man Asian Literary Prize, the Walter Scott Prize, and a Booker Prize shortlisting (Wikipedia, n.d.-b).

Also known for: The Gift of Rain · The House of Doors

Why start here: Set in 1949 Malaya, it follows Yun Ling Teoh, a survivor of a Japanese wartime camp, as she apprentices under a mysterious former royal gardener to build a memorial garden for her sister. Lyrical and slow-burning, it won the 2012 Man Asian Literary Prize and 2013 Walter Scott Prize and was shortlisted for the Booker (The Booker Prizes, n.d.).

Photo: Goodreads

Zen Cho

Fantasy Lovers

Born in Subang Jaya, Selangor and now based in the UK, Cho holds a law degree from Cambridge and has won the Crawford, British Fantasy, and Hugo Awards (Wikipedia, n.d.-c).

Also known for: Black Water Sister · Spirits Abroad

Why start here: Described by Cho herself as "Jane Austen with dragons and people of colour," Sorcerer to the Crown drops a freed slave turned magician into Regency London's magical establishment. It's witty, socially sharp, and won the British Fantasy Award for Best Newcomer (Herrick District Library, n.d.; Options, The Edge, 2023).

Photo: © Darren Johnson / PRH

Preeta Samarasan

Family Drama

Born and raised in Ipoh, Samarasan earned her MFA at the University of Michigan and now lives in France, where she remains a frequent commentator on Malaysian race relations (AGNI, n.d.).

Debut novel, an early version of which won the Hopwood Award

Why start here: Told out of chronological order, Evening Is the Whole Day peels back the secrets of one wealthy Malaysian Indian family across three generations, circling the 1969 riots. The New York Times Book Review compared Samarasan's prose to Eudora Welty's for how it handles family, race, and class (BookBrowse, 2008).

Photo: eksentrika.com, sourced from Preeta Samarasan's Facebook page

Zen Cho - start with Sorcerer to the Crown

Cho blends period English fantasy with Malaysian folklore and a sharp sense of humour about who gets to belong in polite society. If you finish it and want something set closer to home, follow up with Black Water Sister, her contemporary Penang-set novel about a young woman haunted by her medium grandmother's ghost (Herrick District Library, n.d.).

Your match

Hanna Alkaf - start with The Weight of Our Sky

A teenage girl with OCD gets separated from her mother during the real 13 May 1969 riots and has to survive the chaos while managing her own mind. It's tense, emotional, and grounded in actual Malaysian history - and it won the Freeman Award for YA/High School Literature (Tatler Asia, n.d.).

Your match

Preeta Samarasan - start with Evening Is the Whole Day

Told in reverse chronology, this novel unpacks one Malaysian Indian family's secrets across three generations - a dismissed servant girl, a runaway daughter, and a patriarch's broken dreams for his country. Critics have compared the prose to Eudora Welty's for its handling of family, race, and class (BookBrowse, 2008).

Your match

Tan Twan Eng - start with The Garden of Evening Mists

A Japanese-camp survivor and former judge apprentices under a mysterious ex-royal gardener to build a memorial garden for her sister in post-war Malaya. Slow, lyrical, and steeped in memory and war - it won the Man Asian Literary Prize and Walter Scott Prize and was Booker-shortlisted (The Booker Prizes, n.d.).

Your match

Tash Aw - start with We, the Survivors

Ah Hock recounts to a journalist how a life of hard, low-wage work led to him killing a migrant labourer. It's a quiet, devastating look at class and the limits of the "Malaysian Dream" in a rapidly modernising country (CBC Radio, 2019).

Part Three · Read

Local Library Recommendations

Now that you've found an author to try, here's where to actually borrow their books - four Malaysian libraries worth the trip, with directions ready to go.

Perpustakaan Kuala Lumpur

Housed in a refurbished 1898 building near Dataran Merdeka and Masjid Jamek, this is Kuala Lumpur's main public library - admission and membership are both free, with 16 branches spread across the city (Wikipedia, n.d.-a; DBKL, n.d.).

  • Large, varied collection across genres and languages
  • Quiet study spaces and discussion rooms
  • Free Wi-Fi, lockers, and a small pantry area
Get Directions

Photo via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Raja Tun Uda Library, Shah Alam

Built as a gift from the Sultan of Selangor rather than a monument, this RM70 million "library in a park" sits beside a golf club and Shah Alam's lake gardens across six storeys (Wikipedia, n.d.-d; Visit Selangor, n.d.).

  • Modern glass architecture and lakeside views
  • Vibrant children's zone and reading lounges
  • An 8-seat 6D theatre - a rare feature for a public library
Get Directions

Photo via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Penang Digital Library

Opened in 2016 in a heritage bungalow on the grounds of Penang Free School, this is a largely bookless, 24-hour space built around thousands of e-books, e-magazines, and journals (Tye, n.d.; The Star, 2025).

  • Open around the clock, every day
  • Fast, reliable Wi-Fi and co-working-style desks
  • An on-site café for when you need a break
Get Directions

Photo via Wikimedia Commons

Sarawak State Library, Kuching

One of Malaysia's most spacious public libraries, set in park-like grounds around a small lake, with a treehouse and playground for younger visitors right outside (Wikipedia, n.d.-e; Malaysia-Traveller, n.d.).

  • Free entry, with members able to borrow up to 10 items
  • A dedicated Sarawakiana collection of local history
  • Regular storytelling sessions and community events
Get Directions

Photo via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Bonus Tip

Many Malaysian libraries now go beyond physical shelves - think e-books, audiobooks, and online learning platforms you can access from home. Sarawak's state library system, for instance, runs its own PANDei e-learning platform alongside its physical branches (Sarawak State Library, n.d.). Check your local library's website to see what's available before you go.

Libraries aren't just for studying.

They're places to discover new stories, learn something new, and connect with your community - the same way a good Malaysian novel can.
